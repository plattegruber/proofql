ProofQL is a review search API: a business (our **customer**) sends us its reviews, and we return the ones relevant to a question so the customer can show them on its own website. This page explains what we store to do that, and what we do not.

## What we collect

- **Account data.** Sign-in is handled by [Clerk](https://clerk.com/legal/privacy); we store the workspace's Clerk organization id, its name, and its plan. We do not store passwords.
- **Reviews the customer imports.** The review text, rating, author display name, author avatar URL, source, date and link, as the customer pushed or uploaded them, plus derived data we compute from them: sentence excerpts, numeric embeddings, a sentiment label. Reviews are **the customer's data**; we process them on the customer's behalf. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm the controller/processor roles with counsel.]
- **Usage and request logs.** For every API request: a request id, the project and key ids, timing, the result count, status codes, and the requesting IP address as recorded by Cloudflare. Query text is **not** logged; its length is. Logs are kept for [PLACEHOLDER retention, e.g. 30 days].
- **Waitlist.** If you leave your email on `/sign-up` before public signup opens, we store that address and when you left it, and use it for one thing: telling you when signup opens.
- **Support email.** Messages you send to <a href={`mailto:${SUPPORT_EMAIL}`}>{SUPPORT_EMAIL}</a> are kept to answer them.

## What we do not do

- We do not sell data, and we do not use reviews to train models. Embeddings are computed by Cloudflare Workers AI and stored only for the customer's own search.
- No large language model reads review text in the current version of the service.
- Review text is only ever returned to callers holding one of **that project's** API keys, and publishable keys are additionally restricted to the customer's listed website origins.

## Google data

When a customer imports its own Google reviews, the import is done with the customer's consent and the reviews are displayed with their author attribution and a Google source badge, as Google's terms require. Where a customer connects a Google account (the Business Profile connector), we store an encrypted refresh token to keep the connection current and delete it when the connection is removed. Retention of Places-sourced content follows Google's Places policies; see the decision recorded in the project's `docs/places.md`. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm the retention position, issue #116.]

The dashboard sets the session cookies Clerk needs to keep you signed in, a short-lived cookie that carries a status message across a page change, and nothing for advertising or cross-site tracking. The embedded snippet sets no cookies on the customer's site.
